# Detroit techno

Soulful, futuristic and warm: machine drums with a human swing, round basslines, and chords or strings that carry hope and melancholy at once. Music first, machines second.

## The feeling

Hopeful and a little sad, like looking at the future from an empty city. The groove swings and breathes; the chords and strings tell the story. Warm analog tones, nothing sterile, nothing aggressive. You dance with your eyes closed.

## The parts

- **Kick**: round and warm, a classic drum-machine kick with some body, not distorted. Words: "warm analog kick", "round 909 kick", "classic drum machine kick".
- **Bass**: a melodic, rubbery bassline or a deep sub that moves with the chords; it plays notes, not just a rumble. Words: "warm analog bassline", "deep round synth bass", "funky sub bass".
- **Hats and ride**: crisp but soft, with swing; a ride cymbal or open hat often carries the energy. Words: "crisp swung hi hat", "909 ride cymbal", "shuffling open hat".
- **Clap or snare**: a clap on 2 and 4, present but not cracking. Words: "classic clap", "soft drum machine clap".
- **Percussion**: congas, toms, rims or shakers with a human, slightly loose feel. Words: "analog conga", "swung rim shot", "soft shaker loop".
- **Music**: the heart: lush pads, string swells, jazzy minor-7th or 9th chords, arpeggios, simple soulful melodies. Words: "lush analog pad", "soulful string chord", "jazzy minor seventh chord", "dreamy synth arpeggio".
- **Vocal**: rare; a spoken word or short soulful phrase, used sparingly. Words: "soulful vocal phrase", "spoken word".
- **Atmosphere (fx)**: tape hiss or vinyl texture only very low, sweeps for transitions. Words: "warm vinyl texture", "spacey sweep".

## Build it in this order

1. Kick, bass and the main chord or string idea together: they define the mood.
2. Clap and hats with swing. Make the groove breathe.
3. A ride or open hat for energy, percussion for movement.
4. A melody or arpeggio that answers the chords, only if the track needs one.

## Mix

- The chords and the bass are as important as the kick; keep the music clearly audible.
- Warm, not bright: soften the highs, a gentle saturation on drums and chords.
- Swing everything a little (hats and percussion more than the kick).
- Around −9 to −7 LUFS; leave some dynamics, the claps and chords should breathe.

## Arrangement

- 6 to 7 minutes. Intro with drums, the chords enter early, a breakdown where the strings or pads swell alone, the drums return with everything.
- Change by adding or filtering parts every 16 bars; let the chord progression evolve slowly.

## Don't

- No harsh distortion, no industrial noise, no hard-techno kicks.
- No stiff, quantised-to-death drums: give them swing and velocity.
- No cheesy major-key trance leads.
- Don't bury the chords under drums.
