# Hard techno (Schranz)

Fast, pounding and relentless: a hammering, distorted kick that drives every beat, rapid-fire loops grinding against each other, and an industrial edge, yet always a groove you can dance to. Schranz is the German name for it and, since the revival, its loudest strand.

## The feeling

A machine at full speed: hypnotic, physical, a little dangerous, and euphoric in a dark way. The kick hits you in the chest every beat and the loops lock into a trance; the crowd moves as one. Raw on purpose, but every part has its place, so it stays powerful instead of noisy. It is not industrial techno: that is slower, colder and stripped back. Hard techno is faster, louder and made for release.

## Where it comes from, and the three strands

- **Classic schranz (mid 1990s to mid 2000s, Germany).** The Frankfurt DJ Chris Liebing coined the word in the mid 1990s for dark, distorted techno; it peaked around 2001 with artists like Sven Wittekind, Marco Remus, Frank Kvitta, Arkus P., Robert Natus, DJ Rush and Mario Ranieri. Its sound: heavily compressed, filtered, chopped percussion loops on top of 909 kicks, snares and hats; almost no melody, at most a single stab or sweep. Afterwards most people simply called it hard techno, often faster than 150 BPM.
- **Hard techno (2010s).** Bigger, cleaner and more distorted kicks with a long, tuned, saturated tail that carries the bass; rave stabs, hoovers and acid lines; builds and drops.
- **The revival (2020s).** Schranz is back on big stages, carried by DJs like Klangkuenstler and Sara Landry and a new generation of German DJs and producers (NIKOLINA, SNTØ, RYX, Instigator, Marco Leckbert). It keeps the rapid-fire loops and the groove but adds a hard-hitting bassline, trance colours and 90s rave nostalgia, and often edits of well-known pop or trance vocals. Sets sit around 150 to 160 BPM.

Pick one strand per track: classic loop schranz, a big-kick hard techno track, or a revival track with a hook. Mixing all three makes noise.

## The parts

- **Kick**: the main event, every beat: hard, punchy and distorted. In loop schranz it's short (the loops fill the space); in hard techno and the revival it has a saturated tail tuned to the key that carries the bass. Words: "hard distorted techno kick", "pounding schranz kick", "saturated rumble kick", "short hard 909 kick".
- **Bass**: usually the kick's tail, or a short, distorted off-beat bass or a rolling bassline between the kicks (the revival's hard-hitting bassline). Never a sustained layer under a long kick tail. Words: "distorted offbeat bass", "rolling hard bassline", "gritty acid bass".
- **Drum loops (drums)**: the heart of schranz: chopped, compressed, filtered percussion loops with a rolling, galloping pattern. Two at most, one low-mid and one higher, moved with filters. Words: "distorted schranz loop", "grinding percussion loop", "filtered hard techno drum loop".
- **Hats**: tight, fast off-beats or 16ths, slightly distorted, or a driving ride. Words: "tight distorted hat", "fast techno hi hat", "driving distorted ride".
- **Clap or snare**: a hard clap on 2 and 4 or only in builds; snare rolls in builds. Words: "hard rave clap", "short hard clap", "snare roll".
- **Percussion**: metallic hits and distorted toms; one layer. Words: "metallic industrial hit", "distorted tom hits".
- **Music**: in loop schranz, a single noisy stab at most; in hard techno and the revival, one short, hooky idea: a rave stab, hoover, acid line, a trance-coloured lead or pad. Repetitive, never a progression. Words: "rave stab", "hoover synth", "acid screech", "dark trance lead", "emotional trance pad".
- **Vocal**: short shouted or chopped rave vocals; in the revival, a chopped hook from a pop or trance vocal. Words: "rave vocal shout", "chopped hard vocal", "female trance vocal hook".
- **Atmosphere (fx)**: filter sweeps on the loops do most of the work; risers and impacts only in builds. Words: "noise riser", "impact hit", "industrial noise sweep".

## Build it in this order

1. The kick (tuned and distorted, with its tail for hard techno and the revival): it should move the room on its own.
2. The main drum loop, locked to the kick; then a second, contrasting loop or the hats.
3. The bass, if the kick tail doesn't already carry it.
4. One hook (stab, lead or vocal) for hard techno and the revival; for classic schranz, filter movement on the loops instead.
5. Clap, builds (snare rolls, risers, rising filters) and the drop, where the track has one.

## Mix

- The kick owns everything below about 150 Hz and is the loudest thing; high-pass the loops and duck anything else with low end.
- Distortion makes highs harsh fast: shelve down leads, hats and loops, and watch 2 to 5 kHz; grit yes, fizz no.
- Very loud masters, around −7 to −4 LUFS, with the kick still punching rather than flattened.

## Arrangement

- 5 to 6 minutes, in 16 and 32 bar blocks that change by swapping, muting or filtering loops.
- Classic schranz: short breaks of a few bars without the kick, never long breakdowns.
- Hard techno and the revival: an intro of kick and loops, a build into the first drop with the hook, a breakdown with the lead or vocal and a riser, a second, bigger drop, a stripped outro.

## Don't

- Don't stack a separate bass under a long kick tail.
- No more than two loops, one percussion layer and one hook at a time.
- No melodic chord progressions; a hook repeats.
- No constant white-noise wash; risers only in builds.
- Don't let distortion turn everything into fizz.
- Don't drift below about 145 BPM: then it's industrial or Berlin techno.

## Listen to

Classic: Chris Liebing ("The Real Schranz" and the Stigmata series), Sven Wittekind, Marco Remus, Frank Kvitta, Arkus P., DJ Rush. Hard techno and the revival: Klangkuenstler ("Weltschmerz", "Die Welt Brennt"), Sara Landry, Trym, NIKOLINA, SNTØ, RYX, Instigator.
